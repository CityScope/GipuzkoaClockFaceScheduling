Network, connection and symmetry rules

Rules used to build the maps (all regions). Values in bold are the parameters. All items confirmed (November 2026).

1. Services

1.1 Only public / publicly subsidised operators (ALSA counts as public in Spain; BlaBlaCar and similar excluded). 1.2 Modes: national railways (red: Renfe, ÖBB, SBB incl. S-Bahn), local railways (green: Euskotren, narrow-gauge, rack railways, trams), regional buses (blue). A rail trip takes the mode of its line (feed + line name, ≥ 80 % of its trips): one trip of a mainline S-Bahn run under another operator's route entry stays red. 1.3 Regional buses only: a bus route must serve ≥ 2 municipalities and have a trip of ≥ 5 km (straight-line sum between stops). Town buses are dropped. 1.4 A route (line) is identified by its GTFS route id (shown by its short name), so different lines with the same number (e.g. two SBB "S3") stay apart. 1.5 Days: a representative weekday, Saturday and Sunday. Time bands: peak 07–09 + 17–19, day 09–17, night 22–06 (whole hours). The map always shows the selected day and band.

2. Nodes

2.1 Candidate stations, per municipality and mode (OpenStreetMap municipality polygons): stops within a 5-min walk (~375 m) of each other form one station. - Rail: every station is a candidate. - Bus: a stop group is a candidate if it is the bus station, lies next to a train station (≤ 500 m), or is served by ≥ 2 regional bus lines (avoids every street corner of a city becoming a candidate). - The main station of a municipality and mode: rail " Hauptbahnhof / Bahnhof" or the stop served by most lines; bus = bus station, or the best-served stop next to the train station. - Each stop belongs to the nearest candidate station of its municipality and mode. 2.1a Every candidate station is judged on its own (2.5): a municipality can end up with no node, one, or several (two important stations of one mode; train and bus station). Names: the municipality, plus the station name when the municipality has several nodes of that mode (e.g. "Linz · Hbf", "Linz · Urfahr"). 2.2 A trip visits a node when it stops within 1 km (rail) / 1.5 km (bus) of the station; the time of the stop closest to the station is used. 2.3 A trip that only passes through the municipality (a stop up to 8 km from the station) serves it on the map (line drawn), but these stops are not station visits: they are not used for connections or symmetry. 2.4 Stations of different modes are merged into one map node when the walk is ≤ 2 min (same building). 2.5 Branching is always required. A station is a node only if it is a branch: it reaches ≥ 3 directions (neighbouring municipalities, or neighbouring stations of the own municipality). Stations within walking distance (any mode, any municipality) count only if together they reach a third direction (train A–X–B next to bus A–X'–B is not a branch). The only exception: a route terminus — a station where all trips start or end, or where ≥ 30 % of the trips of some route that reach the station start or end there. A route terminus is always a node, also outside the region (so a route leaving the region is drawn up to its end), and is never removed by the 2-min rule. Nothing else justifies a node (no "line end ≥ 10 %", no "bypassed important stop" exceptions). Non-branch stations are removed and their neighbours linked directly. 2.5a Redundant stations. A branching station is kept only if it offers at least one change between two lines that cannot be made at any other node (lines of stations within walking distance count as its own). If every pair of its lines also meets at another node, it is removed and its neighbours are linked directly — e.g. Buttisholz (lines 60/61/62 all also stop at Ruswil: Ruswil now links straight to Ettiswil and Sursee), Werthenstein (its train and bus lines also meet at Malters / Wolhusen). Removal order: smaller municipality first (WorldPop population of the municipality), secondary stations of a town before its main one, then fewer departures; re-checked after each removal, then the branch rule is applied again. A route terminus is kept as a node only if the last stretch of that route is not also run by other trips that continue beyond it (Buttisholz: line 60 ends there, but 61/62 continue). 2.5b Every route stays drawable: after all removals, a route whose trips mostly reach fewer than 2 nodes, or that serves the region but has no node inside it, gets its end stations back as nodes. 2.6 Outside municipalities appear only as hubs with ≥ 150 departures a day, or as route termini (2.5).

3. Links (map lines)

3.1 A link joins consecutive node visits of the same trip and mode. 3.2 Links shorter than 2 min are collapsed (the less important node is removed). Links shorter than 10 min are kept only if both stations are needed for connections (a timed connection or a symmetry in some time band); otherwise the less important station (smaller municipality, fewer departures) is removed — also an end station — and its neighbours are linked directly. Two stations of the same mode in the same municipality less than 10 min apart are one place: the secondary one is removed whatever its connections (e.g. Donostia Urbieta / Etxaide next to the bus station). Repeated until stable; a line always keeps at least two nodes. 3.3 Implausible legs (straight-line speed above the mode maximum, detours much slower than the route's usual time) are removed. A far visit (2.3) has no station position, so its speed is not checked; a far visit in the middle of a trip that is off the way (passing it is more than 1.3 × the direct distance) is not a link stop. 3.5 One connected network. The map is one connected graph (links + walking links). A station is never removed (3.2) if that would split the network; a piece that is cut off gets back the station that links it to the rest (a station of one of its municipalities, or within walking distance, served by trips that also reach the main network). 3.6 Two-way links. Links should be two-way. For a link a → b with no service b → a: if the return trips go b → x (→ y) → a and that path takes at most 1.4 × + 4 min and is on the way (at most 1.8 × the straight-line distance + 1 km), the a → b trips are drawn along it (passing x, y without stopping); if the return trips use a sister station of the same municipality and mode, the trips are drawn between those two stations; otherwise one-way legs with at most 3 trips/day are exceptional and dropped — unless they are the only service of their line, which then stays one-way. The decisions are listed in output/<region>/oneway_links.csv. 3.4 Night view: a link is shown only if it has a service between 00:00 and 05:00.

4. Headways

4.1 pyGTFSHandler formula, all services of the measured stream pooled together (never one headway per line merged afterwards): headway = (first gap² + Σ gaps²) / window, summed over the windows of the band, with first gap = (first − start) + (end − last). 4.2 Edge headways are given per direction: in = arrivals from that edge, out = departures to that edge. 4.3 Frequent = headway under 15 min (e.g. 14.8 min is frequent; exactly 15 min is not). 4.4 One-off or few exceptional services barely change the formula, so they need no special treatment.

5. Edges

5.1 An edge of a node = one direction out of the node: towards one neighbouring town, for one mode, at one station (own station or a station reached on foot). 5.2 Fast / slow edges (at most two edges per direction): the services of an edge are split into a fast and a slow group only if - the slow group makes ≥ 2 more stops and takes more than 10 min longer (median travel time to the same next node), and - each group has ≥ 6 trips a day (a few exceptional fast services do not form a group). There is no condition on the headways of the two groups. Otherwise all services form one edge. When split, the edge is treated as two different edges ("Town (fast)", "Town (slow)") in all computations below and in the node popup. The map does not draw a second line or an extra node. 5.3 Fast services that skip the next node altogether reach another neighbouring town and therefore are already a different edge (e.g. Sursee: IR to Luzern vs S-Bahn to Nottwil). Changes between such edges in the same travel direction are treated like any other pair of edges.

6. Walking

6.1 Walking transfer only up to 5 min (75 m/min, straight line × 1.3), between any two stations (any mode, any municipality). 6.2 Changing mode or station needs only the real walk time (no extra penalty).

7. Measuring a relation (direction A → B at a node) — real timetable, all services

7.1 Every arrival from A in the band takes the first departure towards B it can reach (plus the real walk time when changing station; no extra penalty). The same vehicle continuing to B — or the same line continuing under a new trip number (e.g. S6 → S6 at Wolhusen) — is a direct trip: wait 0. 7.2 Intelligently: when several arrivals catch the same departure, only the latest counts (nobody arrives earlier just to wait longer). 7.3 From these pairs: the average wait of the relation, and its headway (formula 4.1 on the departures reached). The changes with a wait ≤ 6 min (≤ 12 min arriving by bus) are its timed changes; Hd = headway of the direct trips, Hc = headway of direct trips + timed changes. 7.4 Pairs of edges that lead the same way (directions less than 45° apart) are not relations: changing between them means going back. They are left out everywhere.

8. Connection (one direction A → B)

8.1 Frequent: the relation itself runs more often than every 15 min (all services) — no coordination needed. Shown as "frequent"; left out of all symmetry shares. 8.2 Direct: only direct trips, or the changes do not make the relation run more often than the direct trips alone (Hc not better than Hd) — no coordination needed. 8.3 Coordinated when all of - the average wait (7.3, direct trips counting 0) is within the limit: ≤ 6 min, ≤ 12 min arriving by bus (rounded to the minute), - it repeats: ≥ 2 timed changes in the band, - the average wait is less than half the headway of the departing edge (otherwise it is what arriving at random gives, e.g. a bus every 20 min with an 11-min wait), - the timed changes are of use: compared with taking any service of the relation (timed or not), keeping only the timed ones may lose headway, but the wait they save must make up for it. Both sides include the direct trips (wait 0). Normal: every departure reached (headway H_all, mean wait W_all, 7.1–7.3). Coordinated: direct trips + timed changes (headway H_t, mean wait W_t). Headways with the formula 4.1. When the relation has direct trips, the timed changes must at least halve the headway of the direct trips (H_t ≤ H_direct / 2), else not coordinated. Then: when the timed services (with the direct trips) are ≥ 80 % of the departures reached there is nothing to compare: coordinated (e.g. Wolhusen → Nebikon at Willisau, 12 of 13). Otherwise coordinated only if (H_t − H_all) / 2 ≤ W_all − W_t (e.g. Arrasate → Zumarraga at Bergara: timed every 39 min instead of 28, no wait saved: not timed). Inside a symmetry the timed changes are those that fit its hand (9.3). The relation table shows H_t and W_t on the first line and H_all and W_all on the second. 8.4 Otherwise: "not timed". 8.5 Weight of a direction, used for every percentage (75 % rule, both-ways / one-way shares, group size): the frequency of the worse of its two edges (60 / its headway = connection opportunities per hour). An hourly relation counts 1, a half-hourly one 2, one every 8 hours 0.125, so rare relations barely count.

9. Symmetry

9.1 Relations needing no coordination (8.1) are ignored. If they make up ≥ 75 % of a node's relations (weighted by the worse edge's headway, 8.4), the node has no symmetry (not needed). 9.2 A symmetry minute (hand) h is a minute of the hour inside every timed change of the symmetry: each of its timed changes arrives at or before h and leaves at or after h (minutes rounded). Arrival and departure times measured from the symmetry minute are therefore never negative. 9.3 A connected direction belongs to a symmetry when ≥ 2 of its timed changes contain one of its hands (it repeats) and these changes, with the direct trips, are of use (8.3, last point). Symmetries are found one after another: the hands h / h + 30 that contain the most connected directions (weighted by frequency), then the most timed changes, then a multiple of 5, then the hand in the middle of the changes; its directions are taken out and the next symmetry (hands ≥ 10 min from the previous ones) is searched among the rest. Only the changes that contain the hand are the timed changes of the direction in that symmetry. A group with ≥ 2 connected directions whose timed changes involve ≥ 3 different neighbouring stations (fast / slow edges and stations reached on foot count once) is a candidate symmetry. A symmetry needs at least one timed change between two non-frequent edges (edge headway ≥ 15 min): a node whose only timed changes involve a frequent line is not a symmetry node. 9.4 Every 30 min (hands h and h + 30, exactly 30 min apart) when each hand contains ≥ 20 % of the fitting changes; otherwise every 60 min at the busier hand. 9.5 Completeness, over the pairs of the group's edges in which at least one direction needs coordination (each pair weighted by the headway of its worse edge, 8.4). A direction works when it is connected in this symmetry or in another symmetry of the same node (e.g. Olten → Zug at :05, Zug → Olten at :53 at Luzern), or needs no coordination (frequent, 8.1, or served directly, 8.1a), or has no service at all (one-way relation). - Complete (solid hand): more than 50 % of the pairs work in both directions. - Half (dotted hand): otherwise, if more than 50 % of the pairs are connected (timed) in at least one direction. - A group whose timed connections run every ≤ 60 min (median headway with changes) always makes the node symmetric (half, or complete if > 50 % works both ways), even below the 50 % one-way share. - Otherwise no symmetry. (Directions served directly or by frequent lines no longer count against completeness: before, a pair whose reverse direction ran by through train made a complete node look half.) 9.6 The largest symmetry (by weight) is drawn on the map symbol; the popup lists all symmetries.

10. Map and popups

10.1 Node popup: symmetries as clickable clock cards (S1, S2, …); selecting one shows only its connections; "show all" returns. 10.1a Node diagram (one spoke per neighbour at its real bearing): solid = works both ways in the selected symmetry, dashed = one way, grey = not in the symmetry, wide light line = frequent (< 15 min). One label on each spoke, in a pill on the line: "◀ min′ ↻headway │ ↻headway min′ ▶" — arrival (arrow towards the node) = minutes before the symmetry minute, departure (arrow away) = minutes after it; ↻ = headway of only the symmetry services of that direction. Numbers on every spoke come from the real timetable (all services, no arrival left out): the mean, over every symmetry minute in the band, of the minutes from the last arrival at or before it and to the first departure at or after it (pulses without a service within one period left out); never negative. A side is coordinated (normal style, with ↻ = headway of its symmetry services, counted in pulses, never better than the symmetry period) when it has timed changes in that symmetry and its mean is within 6 min rail / 12 min bus (plus the real walk of its changes when they need one); arriving or leaving at the symmetry minute itself (0 min) is part of the symmetry; a direction running more often than every 15 min is frequent: it counts as working (normal style; a spoke working both ways this way or timed is solid) and the spoke gets the frequent highlight (wide light line); otherwise grey italics. A divider │ only between a coordinated and a non-coordinated side; when both ways have symmetry headways less than 5 min apart, ↻ is shown once, in the middle. Under the diagram: the main station, then each other station of the node with its walk from the main one. Under the name, in grey: headway of the edge, all services ("arr′/dep′" when they differ); at most 3 line names (short form) + "+n". Directions served at a walk partner (another node within 5 min walk) are drawn only in the symmetries that use them, with "🚶 X′" after the name. Labels are placed without overlaps: the spoke labels move outward until they touch no other label or spoke (grey ones that cannot be placed cleanly are left out); the names are stacked in one column per side, with a thin leader line when moved from the spoke tip. 10.2 Edge connections table: Edge A | ← | → | Edge B, ordered rail–rail, rail–local rail, local–local, rail–bus, local–bus, bus–bus; within each, connected first, then by wait. Each edge shows its in / out headways; fast / slow edges are labelled. Each direction shows two lines: (1) with timing: mean wait (+ walk) of the timed changes and the headway of timed changes + direct services — or "not timed" / "frequent" / "direct"; (2) all services, real timetable: every arrival takes the first departure towards the other edge, the same vehicle continuing counts with wait 0; (3) if there are direct services: their headway; when several arrivals catch the same departure only the latest counts; mean wait (+ walk) and headway of these trips. 10.3 Route connections table: the same layout for the lines involved in the counted changes (route · town), with number of changes, mean wait, walk and symmetry tag. 10.4 Edge (link) popup: travel times, services, lines, longest wait, and one box per direction with its headway and departure minutes. 10.5 Legend: number of symmetry nodes in the selected day / band; ⓘ panel with these rules in short and an animation of a pulse at an invented station; "Coordinated only" keeps only the links that belong to a symmetry group (at either end, selected day / band) and hides nodes left without links. In the video, "Coordinated only" shows only vehicles on those links whose trip takes part in a timed change of a symmetry group. 10.6 Video: vehicles appear ≤ 0.5 s before moving and disappear ≤ 0.5 s after stopping; stopped ≤ 10 min shown, never longer than the best line's headway at the station; "Hide frequent lines" (≤ 14 min, per link and band) and "Coordinated only" checkboxes.
